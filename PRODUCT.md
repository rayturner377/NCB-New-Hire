# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Human Resources officers, administrators, medical doctors, clinicians, and patients use this system to manage pre-employment medical onboarding. Most users are in task-focused workflows: creating patients and medicals, completing assigned forms, reviewing doctor assessments, managing offices and users, and reporting on status, billing, and workload.

## Product Purpose

The platform manages the full medical onboarding case lifecycle: HR creates a patient and medical case, the patient completes required intake and consent, the medical office completes the physician assessment, HR reviews the completed medical, and billing can be tracked through paid or unpaid status. Success means each role can see only the work they need, complete it quickly, and trust that case status, signatures, billing, and audit information are accurate.

## Brand Personality

Calm, professional, precise. The interface should feel like a modern medical operations system with familiar controls, clear feedback, restrained use of brand color, and little visual noise.

## Anti-references

Avoid AI-generated dashboard clutter, oversized cards, decorative medical imagery, marketing-style hero sections inside the app, over-rounded components, and tables that hide important actions or require horizontal wandering. Avoid exposing confidential medical details in places meant only for operational summaries.

## Design Principles

1. Put the workflow first: every screen should make the next case action obvious.
2. Separate concepts clearly: patient profile, medical case, doctor assessment, billing, and reporting should not blur together.
3. Show the right amount of data: summaries for scanning, drill-downs for detail, and sensitive medical content only where appropriate.
4. Keep controls familiar: tables, filters, tabs, buttons, modals, and status labels should behave consistently across roles.
5. Design for scale: searches, filters, summaries, and reports should remain usable when the system contains many users, offices, doctors, and medical cases.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA for contrast, keyboard access, focus states, and readable form controls. Motion should be brief, purposeful, and respect reduced-motion preferences. Reporting charts must be supported by readable labels, totals, and tables so chart color is not the only source of meaning.
